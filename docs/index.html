Free and open source (MIT). It doesn't touch Steam, your games or your account.

How it works

Steam already knows how to use a cache. Steam Boost is one.

Steam can download through a cache on your local network, a feature built for LAN parties. Steam Boost is that cache, on your own PC, and it keeps nothing: it fetches each piece over whichever of your connections will finish it first.

  1. 1

    Steam looks for a cache

    Steam Boost adds one line to your hosts file, so the cache Steam finds when it starts is Steam Boost.

  2. 2

    Steam Boost splits the work

    Steam asks for a game in pieces of about a megabyte. Each goes to the connection that will finish it soonest, so a slower phone carries its share without holding up the rest.

  3. 3

    Steam checks every piece

    Pieces arrive encrypted, and Steam checks each against its checksum, so nothing Steam Boost does can change a game. If Steam Boost stops, Steam goes back to its own servers.

Features

Built to stay out of your way

  • Each piece on the fastest line

    Every piece of a download goes to the connection that will finish it first, judged from each one's speed, delay and backlog. A connection that falters is rested.

  • A daily cap for your phone

    Give a connection a daily limit. Steam Boost stops using it at the limit, carries on over the others, and starts counting again at midnight.

  • Every connection, live

    See each connection's speed as it downloads, its share of the whole, and what it has used of its daily cap.

  • One click to set up, one to undo

    Setup adds one line to your hosts file behind one Windows prompt, then restarts Steam. Undo setup takes the line out again.

  • Quiet in the tray

    Close the window and the boost keeps going. Steam Boost can start with Windows, so it's there whenever Steam downloads.

  • It can't harm a game

    Steam checks every piece against its checksum, so a bad one is thrown away, never installed. Your account and your games stay as they are.

What you need

  • Two connections, each with its own internet. Home broadband plus your phone's mobile data over a USB cable is the usual pair. Wi-Fi and Ethernet to the same router share one line, so together they add nothing.
  • A Windows 10 or 11 PC with Steam. The macOS and Linux builds are experimental.
  • Administrator permission, once, for the hosts-file line.

What it changes on your PC

  • One line in your hosts file: 127.0.0.1 lancache.steamcontent.com. Undo setup takes it out.
  • A place for Steam to ask: it listens on 127.0.0.1, ports 80 and 443, where only this PC can reach it.
  • No account and no tracking. Besides Steam's own downloads, it only contacts GitHub, to see if there's a newer version.

Questions

Common questions

Is this allowed by Steam?

Steam Boost uses Steam's built-in support for LAN caches, the feature LAN parties and tools like LANCache rely on. It doesn't change Steam, your games, your account or anti-cheat; it only changes which of your connections each piece of a download comes over. Steam Boost isn't made or endorsed by Valve.

Will it use up my mobile data?

It uses as much as your phone can carry, and a game can be 50 GB or more. Set a daily cap on the phone's connection in the app, a little below what your plan allows: the plan counts some overhead that Steam Boost can't see.

Why does it ask for administrator permission?

To add one line to your hosts file that points Steam's cache name (lancache.steamcontent.com) at your PC. Nothing else needs it, and Undo setup takes the line out again.

My download didn't get faster.

Check that both connections show a speed in the app. If Steam Boost says Steam hasn't asked it for anything, exit Steam from its menu (Steam, then Exit) and open it again: Steam only looks for a cache when it starts. Steam's own bandwidth limit (Settings, then Downloads) still applies, and a slow disk can be the limit while Steam installs.

Does Steam Boost collect any data?

No. There's no account and no tracking. Besides Steam's own downloads, the only thing it contacts is GitHub, to see if there's a newer version. Its settings and logs stay on your PC.

How do I undo it?

Click Undo setup in the app's settings and restart Steam. Do that before you uninstall, so the hosts-file line goes too.

Does it work with a second Wi-Fi, or a hotspot?

Any two adapters that each reach the internet another way will do: a USB Wi-Fi adapter on a phone's hotspot alongside your built-in Wi-Fi on home broadband, say. One Wi-Fi adapter can only join one network at a time.
