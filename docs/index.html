Steam Boost
How it works Download Questions GitHub

Every connection you have, on one Steam download.

Steam Boost splits Steam's downloads across your Wi-Fi, Ethernet and phone, piece by piece, so a game arrives at their combined speed.

Download for Windows Other platforms

Free and open source. This is a pre-release for Windows 10 and 11; macOS and Linux builds are experimental.

How it works

  1. Steam looks for a cache

    Steam can download through a cache on your local network, a feature built for LAN parties. Steam Boost adds one line to your hosts file, so the cache Steam finds is Steam Boost, on your own PC.

  2. Steam Boost splits the work

    Steam asks for a game in pieces of about a megabyte. Each piece goes to the connection that will finish it soonest, so a slower phone still carries its share without holding up the rest.

  3. Steam checks every piece

    Pieces arrive encrypted, and Steam checks each one against its checksum, so nothing Steam Boost does can change a game. If Steam Boost stops, Steam goes back to its own servers.

What you need

Two connections, each with its own internet. Your home Wi-Fi plus your phone's mobile data over a USB cable is the usual pair. Wi-Fi and Ethernet to the same router share one line, so together they add nothing.

A Windows 10 or 11 PC with Steam installed, and administrator permission once, for the hosts-file line.

A daily cap for your phone's data

Give the phone a daily cap. Steam Boost stops using it once the day's cap is reached, carries on over your other connections, and starts counting again at midnight.

Windows may say it protected your PC, because the app isn't code-signed yet. Choose More info, then Run anyway. The source is on GitHub if you'd rather build it yourself.

On a Mac, macOS blocks the app the first time it opens, for the same reason. Go to System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. If it says the app is damaged, run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Steam Boost.app" in Terminal, then open it again.

Questions

Is this allowed by Steam?

Steam Boost uses Steam's built-in support for LAN caches, the feature LAN parties and tools like LANCache rely on. It doesn't change Steam, your games, your account or anti-cheat; it only changes which of your connections each piece of a download comes over. Steam Boost isn't made or endorsed by Valve.

Will it use up my mobile data?

It uses as much as your phone can carry, and a game can be 50 GB or more. Set a daily cap on the phone's connection in the app, a little below what your plan allows: the plan counts some overhead that Steam Boost can't see.

Why does it ask for administrator permission?

To add one line to your hosts file that points Steam's cache name (lancache.steamcontent.com) at your PC. Nothing else needs it, and Undo setup takes the line out again.

My download didn't get faster.

Check that both connections show a speed in the app. If Steam Boost says Steam hasn't asked it for anything, exit Steam from its menu (Steam, then Exit) and open it again: Steam only looks for a cache when it starts. Steam's own bandwidth limit (Settings, then Downloads) still applies, and a slow disk can be the limit while Steam installs.

How do I undo it?

Click Undo setup in the app's settings and restart Steam. Do that before you uninstall, so the hosts-file line goes too.

Does it work with a second Wi-Fi, or a hotspot?

Any two adapters that each reach the internet another way will do: a USB Wi-Fi adapter on a phone's hotspot alongside your built-in Wi-Fi on home broadband, say. One Wi-Fi adapter can only join one network at a time.
